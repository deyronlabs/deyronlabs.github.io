## What happened

Reuters reported on 18 November 2026 that NVIDIA is no longer shipping licensed Hopper-generation data center products to China. According to Reuters, interim CEO Colette Kress said so on the company's earnings call on Tuesday 17 November, in the words "we are no longer shipping licenced Hopper data centre products to China", and said NVIDIA's guidance does not include such products. The statement is Reuters' account of the call. We have not read NVIDIA's transcript or filing, so the wording above is as Reuters quotes it.

## Key details

- **What was said (per Reuters):** Kress told analysts NVIDIA sold $2.4 billion of licensed Hopper data center products to China in the quarter that ended 25 October 2026, and that these products are no longer being shipped. Hopper includes the H200.
- **Reason and duration:** Reuters says Kress did not explain why shipments stopped or whether the halt is permanent.
- **Background (per Reuters):** the U.S. relaxed its H200 licensing policy in January, with applications reviewed case by case, and a 25% tariff applies to eligible chips. Reuters says 10 Chinese companies, including Alibaba, Tencent and ByteDance, were approved to buy them, that Beijing has restricted imports of the H200, and, in earlier reporting in November, that about 600,000 H200 chips were stored in Hong Kong.
- **Consistent with NVIDIA's results release:** as we reported, NVIDIA's fourth-quarter outlook of $130.0 billion assumes no Data Center compute revenue from China.
- **Not confirmed:** NVIDIA's own transcript or filing for this statement, the reason for the halt, whether it covers products other than licensed Hopper parts, and whether it is permanent. We did not open the Bloomberg or The Verge reports on the same news.

## Why it matters

For China-based buyers of NVIDIA hardware, the report points to a supply that had been approved by the U.S. but is no longer shipping from NVIDIA's side, which changes what chips they can plan around. For everyone else, NVIDIA's guidance already excludes China data center compute, so the practical question is why shipments stopped, which NVIDIA has not publicly explained in the account Reuters gives. Treat this as a Reuters-attributed report until NVIDIA's transcript or a filing confirms the wording.
