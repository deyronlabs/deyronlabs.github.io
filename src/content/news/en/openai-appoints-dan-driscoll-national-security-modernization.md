## What happened

OpenAI announced on 2 November 2026 that Dan Driscoll will join the company as Senior Counselor for National Security Modernization. OpenAI describes it as a newly created role in which he will work with senior leadership and external partners to advise on how advanced AI can strengthen national and cyber security, with a particular focus on working with U.S. allies on responsible use.

## Key details

Everything below is from OpenAI's announcement, read on 4 November 2026. The page also carries statements from Sam Altman and from Driscoll; we read those in a machine-translated version of the page and do not quote them here.

- **The role:** according to OpenAI, Driscoll will work with OpenAI teams and leaders across governments, industry, academia and allied institutions on practical modernization initiatives that support what it calls the buildout of democratic AI. He will also help internal teams understand the operational needs of national security institutions.
- **His background (OpenAI's description):** he was the 26th Secretary of the United States Army, overseeing operations, modernization and resources for nearly one million Active, Guard and Reserve soldiers and more than 265,000 Army civilians. OpenAI describes him as a former Army officer and business leader with experience in military service, law and the private sector.
- **Advisory scope:** OpenAI says he will advise its public and private sector partners on modernizing policy and strategy choices so AI can be used practically for national security.
- **Link to Daybreak:** OpenAI says the appointment builds on its work with allies and partners on cyber resilience under its Daybreak program.
- **Not stated:** the announcement does not give a start date, compensation, or any new product, contract or government agreement.

## Why it matters

The announcement is about people and positioning, not a product: it adds a senior figure with a defense background to OpenAI's government-facing work, and signals that the company is putting more weight on national security and allied governments as customers and partners. Nothing in the post changes what OpenAI's products do or cost for existing users.

Whether the role leads to concrete programs, such as new government deals or cyber initiatives, is not answered by the post, so those would need to be confirmed when and if OpenAI announces them.
