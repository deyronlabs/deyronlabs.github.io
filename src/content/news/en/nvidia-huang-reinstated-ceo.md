## What happened

On 23 November 2026 Nvidia announced that its board "voted unanimously" to reinstate Jensen Huang as president, chief executive officer and a member of the board, "effective immediately". Huang resigned from all three roles on 12 November. Six days before the announcement, on 17 November, Nvidia's chair Harvey C. Jones had said, per CBS News, that the board did not intend to rehire him (see our [earlier report](/en/news/nvidia-huang-return-statement/)).

Nvidia's press release says the board acted "following a series of discussions and meetings" and was satisfied with Huang's candor and ability to lead. We read the release on Nvidia's newsroom and the copy attached to the company's Form 8-K. The 8-K reports the earliest event date as 22 November and is signed 23 November by Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress, who led the company as interim CEO and, per the release, continues as CFO. Harvey C. Jones remains board chair.

## Key details

- **Government input:** the release says "the Board received input from senior levels of the United States government, and based on that input, the Board is confident that Mr. Huang's reinstatement poses no national security risk." The release does not name any official or agency. This is the board's statement, not a government finding.
- **Investigation continues:** "in light of recent reporting that Mr. Huang has been the target of a foreign intelligence effort", the board formed a committee of solely independent directors, which retained independent counsel. Nvidia says the investigation will proceed to conclusion and that Huang fully supports it. We have not seen any findings.
- **New oversight:** the board has formed a National Security and Export Control Committee of solely independent directors to oversee trade compliance and national security risks, and is engaging counsel with national security experience. Nvidia is also starting a search for a new Chief Compliance Officer, who would report to the Nominating and Corporate Governance Committee.
- **Government inquiries:** Nvidia says it will keep cooperating with ongoing government investigations and inquiries. Our earlier coverage of [California's subpoena](/en/news/nvidia-california-subpoena/) and the [board's national security committee](/en/news/nvidia-board-national-security-export-control-committee/) describes some of them.
- **Quotes:** Jones: "The Board believes Jensen is the right person to lead NVIDIA." Huang: "I am honored to return to NVIDIA and lead one of the most consequential technology companies in the world at the beginning of a new industrial revolution."
- **Political comment:** the independent archive trumpstruth.org lists two 23 November posts by President Trump about the decision, which it timestamps 9:07 AM and 9:50 AM (time zone not stated). Per the archive's listing, the first says the board was right to return Huang, that the government sees no national security risk and that Nvidia should continue its internal export-controls compliance review; the second calls it a "great decision". We read the archive's list page, not the individual posts, and could not open the originals on Truth Social.
- **Not confirmed here:** the reasons for Huang's 12 November resignation, what the investigation covers beyond the board's description, and whether Huang's reported 1 December appearance before the House (from CBS, per our earlier article) still stands. We did not verify share-price moves on 23 November.

## Why it matters

Nvidia supplies most of the accelerators behind AI training and inference, so the identity of its CEO, and the conditions attached to his return, matter to cloud providers and labs that plan hardware purchases years ahead. The release pairs the reinstatement with a new export-controls committee and a compliance chief, which signals that the board expects scrutiny of trade compliance to continue.

The open questions are concrete: whether the independent investigation publishes findings, how the government inquiries described in the release proceed, and who is hired as Chief Compliance Officer. Until then, the "no national security risk" assessment rests on the board's account of unnamed government input.
