## What happened

According to CBS News, Jensen Huang made his first public statement since resigning as Nvidia's president, CEO and director on 12 November 2026. CBS describes it as a statement issued on the morning of Tuesday 17 November and quotes him as saying: "I did not live up to the standards expected of everyone at Nvidia, and I have made serious mistakes in my personal life." He is quoted as adding that he is "committed to cooperating with the board's independent investigation."

On a possible return, CBS quotes Huang: "If the board of directors decides that my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the company and America, I would be honored to answer these calls and return as CEO."

That evening, CBS reports, Nvidia chair Harvey Jones said the board does not intend to rehire Huang, that a search for a new chief executive is under way with internal and external candidates, and that the board has "every confidence" in interim CEO Colette Kress. We could not read a transcript of Nvidia's 17 November earnings call, so Jones's wording comes from CBS, not from Nvidia.

## Key details

- **Source of the quotes:** CBS News. We did not find Huang's original statement on Nvidia's newsroom or investor site, and CBS does not name the channel, so every quote here is attributed to CBS and we have not independently confirmed the wording.
- **Board position:** per CBS, no plan to reinstate Huang; the CEO search continues. This matches the 12 November announcement that the board would consider internal and external candidates for a permanent CEO.
- **Congress:** CBS reports that Huang has agreed to appear before the House of Representatives on 1 December. We did not find the official House schedule, so the date is CBS's.
- **Context CBS reports:** a reinstatement petition with more than 190,000 signatures; a 16 November comment attributed to President Trump that he "would not mind seeing" Huang return; Nvidia shares down about 1.7% to $153.51 and roughly 16% since reports about Huang surfaced; analyst Dan Ives calling a return unlikely.
- **What we have not confirmed:** the circumstances behind Huang's resignation and his reference to his personal life. Nvidia's 12 November release gave no reason, and the board's independent investigation, led by outside counsel, has not published findings.
- **Related coverage:** our earlier articles on the [resignation](/en/news/nvidia-ceo-huang-resigns/), the [board's investigation](/en/news/huang-megaspeed-congress/) and the [Q3 FY27 results](/en/news/nvidia-q3-fy27-results-record-revenue-kress-interim-ceo/).

## Why it matters

Nvidia supplies most of the accelerators behind AI training and inference, so who runs it, and under what scrutiny, matters to cloud providers and labs that plan multi-year hardware purchases. Two public positions now exist side by side: Huang says he would return if asked, and the board, per CBS, says it will not ask. Until the investigation reports and a permanent CEO is named, Kress remains in charge and the open questions are the board's timeline and what the 1 December appearance covers.
