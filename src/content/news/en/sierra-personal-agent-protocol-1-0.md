## What happened

On 13 November 2026 (17:00 UTC) Sierra published "Releasing Personal Agent Protocol 1.0", written by Bret Taylor and Clay Bavor. The Personal Agent Protocol, nicknamed Poppy, sets out how a person's own AI agent interacts with companies through their business agents, websites and APIs. Sierra first announced the protocol with Meta in October, when only a v0.1 specification was promised (covered earlier on this site).

## Key details

From Sierra's post, read on 14 November 2026. We have not read the specification itself.

- **Design:** built largely on existing standards, namely MCP, OpenAPI and UCP, with authentication through OAuth.
- **Availability:** version 1.0 is "now available" at personalagentprotocol.org, with an implementation kit and validator for companies.
- **Backing, per Sierra:** initial design partners Meta, Genesys, Instinct, NiCE, Rocket, Shopify, Stripe and Walmart; 60 more joined, bringing the total past 100, including Salesforce, Cisco, Delta, Intuit and Zoom.
- **Live now, per Sierra:** Shopify has enabled Poppy for every merchant on its platform, it is on for all users of Meta's Muse, and Mercury, Synchrony and Turkish Airlines are live design partners.
- **Not stated:** any usage numbers or independent confirmation of the partner claims.

## Why it matters

As agents start to shop and book for people, companies need a way to tell a legitimate personal agent from a bot, and users need control over what it does. Sierra's protocol is one attempt at a shared answer, and the named partners give it more weight than most early standards. This week also saw OpenAI launch ChatGPT Wallet for agent purchases and United Airlines move to block unauthorized AI agents, so the topic is moving quickly.

Whether it becomes a standard depends on adoption beyond the design partners, which is not yet visible. The partner and live-deployment claims are Sierra's and should be read that way.
