## What happened

In its report on 20 November 2026 about a global fall in share prices, The Guardian wrote that Nvidia's shares fell by 7% after California served the chip company with a subpoena as part of an investigation into whether its advanced semiconductors ended up in China.

Update, 23 November 2026: Bloomberg reported on 21 November that the California investigation into alleged illegal chip sales to China, and into whether investors were misled, is also looking at possible criminal violations of state law. According to Bloomberg, Attorney General Rob Bonta said on 21 November that several California laws could be violated if Nvidia or its former CEO Jensen Huang gave the public false information about such sales, and the inquiry is at a preliminary stage with no conclusions. We could read only the opening of that report, which is behind a paywall, and we have not found the attorney general's own statement.

For the original 20 November report, that single sentence was all we had. We tried to confirm it on the California attorney general's news page, but could not read it, and we found no subpoena, no official announcement and no statement from Nvidia. The report is therefore unconfirmed: we do not know which California authority issued the subpoena, what it asks for, or when it was served.

## Key details

- **What is reported (The Guardian):** a California subpoena to Nvidia, part of an investigation into whether its advanced chips ended up in China; Nvidia shares down 7% on 20 November.
- **Added 23 November (Bloomberg):** the inquiry also covers possible criminal law violations under California law; it was announced on 20 November and is preliminary, with no conclusions. This comes from a news report, not a document, and we read only its opening.
- **What we could not find:** the issuing authority's own statement, the date the subpoena was served, the scope of the request, any copy of the subpoena, and a response from Nvidia. Bloomberg's phrase "former CEO" for Huang is its wording; we have not checked it against a company filing.
- **The share fall is not a measure of the subpoena:** the Guardian's article is about a broad market slump that day. It reports the Nasdaq down 2.9% and the S&P 500 down 1.66% in morning trading and large falls in Asian markets, so the 7% drop cannot be put down to the subpoena alone.

## Why it matters

Nvidia's advanced chips are the subject of U.S. export controls, and a state-level inquiry into where they end up would be a new angle if it is confirmed. For now the practical point is narrow: a single news report says it happened, and none of the details that would show what the investigation covers has been published.

A possible criminal dimension raises the stakes of the inquiry, but a preliminary investigation does not mean charges will follow, and the legal theories described so far are about what was said to the public and investors. We will update this article if the attorney general's office, Nvidia or a court document confirms or corrects the reports.
