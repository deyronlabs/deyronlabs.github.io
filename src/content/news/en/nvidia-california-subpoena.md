## What happened

In its report on 20 November 2026 about a global fall in share prices, The Guardian wrote that Nvidia's shares fell by 7% after California served the chip company with a subpoena as part of an investigation into whether its advanced semiconductors ended up in China.

That sentence is all we have. We tried to confirm it on the California attorney general's news page, but could not read it, and we found no subpoena, no official announcement and no statement from Nvidia. The report is therefore unconfirmed: we do not know which California authority issued the subpoena, what it asks for, or when it was served.

## Key details

- **What is reported (The Guardian):** a California subpoena to Nvidia, part of an investigation into whether its advanced chips ended up in China; Nvidia shares down 7% on 20 November.
- **What we could not find:** the issuing authority, the date, the scope of the request, any copy of the subpoena, and a response from Nvidia.
- **The share fall is not a measure of the subpoena:** the Guardian's article is about a broad market slump that day. It reports the Nasdaq down 2.9% and the S&P 500 down 1.66% in morning trading and large falls in Asian markets, so the 7% drop cannot be put down to the subpoena alone.

## Why it matters

Nvidia's advanced chips are the subject of U.S. export controls, and a state-level inquiry into where they end up would be a new angle if it is confirmed. For now the practical point is narrow: a single news report says it happened, and none of the details that would show what the investigation covers has been published.

We will update this article if the issuing office, Nvidia or a court document confirms or corrects the report.
