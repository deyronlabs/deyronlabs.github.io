## What happened

### Anthropic: Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2

On 9 November Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2, which it describes as the same underlying model. Fable carries strong cyber and biology safeguards and is open to everyone; Mythos goes to verified organizations. Fable 5.2 costs $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens, 20% below Fable 5.1, with output up to 30% faster, a 1M-token context window and up to 128K tokens of output. Anthropic reports 49.1% on FrontierCode 2.0 against 32.4% for Fable 5.1; we could not read the system card and found no independent results. An introductory usage offer on Pro and standard Team plans runs through 16 November. ([full article](/en/news/claude-fable-5-2/))

### Google: Gemini 4 Carbon and Hydrogen

Gemini 4 Carbon became generally available on 10 November, two weeks after launching for cybersecurity defenders. The introductory price is $2 and $10 per million input and output tokens through 30 November, then $4 and $20 from 1 December. Google says Carbon ranks first on CursorBench 4.0 and Zapier's AutomationBench at more than 2x lower cost per task than the next best external model, and that Argon remains the better choice for most work. ([full article](/en/news/google-gemini-4-carbon-general-availability/)) On 12 November Google launched Gemini 4 Hydrogen at $1.50 and $7.50 per million tokens with a flat 1M-token context window, and says it scores 51 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index against 50 for Argon. Neither price was on Google's public pricing page when we checked, so confirm it in AI Studio. ([full article](/en/news/google-gemini-4-hydrogen/))

### Anthropic IPO: from filing to roadshow

On 2 November Anthropic filed an amended S-1 with third-quarter revenue of $16.96 billion and about $895 billion of committed spend, substantially all for compute and infrastructure. On 10 November it launched the roadshow: 825 million Class A shares at an expected $70 to $78, to list on Nasdaq under ANT. By our own arithmetic that is roughly $58 billion to $64 billion before fees. The registration statement is not yet effective, so the price and terms can still change. ([full article](/en/news/anthropic-s1-ipo-filing-nasdaq-ant/))

### Nvidia: Jensen Huang resigns

Nvidia's board said on 12 November that Jensen Huang has resigned as president, CEO and director, effective immediately. CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO and Harvey Jones, a former Synopsys CEO, becomes the board's first chair. The release gives no reason, and we do not repeat unconfirmed press reports about the cause. Two days earlier, independent board members said they had retained a former acting United States attorney and Wachtell, Lipton for an independent investigation; Nvidia has not said whether the two are connected. Third-quarter results are still scheduled for 17 November. ([full article](/en/news/nvidia-ceo-huang-resigns/))

### Models and prices

- **Muse Ember (Meta):** its smallest model, for agent work, at $0.10 and $0.50 per million tokens with a 1M-token context window; Meta did not say whether the weights are open. ([full article](/en/news/meta-muse-ember/))
- **Qwen4-Max-Preview (Alibaba):** a 1M-token preview open only to Token Plan subscribers until 25 November, with no price, weights or benchmarks published. ([full article](/en/news/alibaba-qwen4-max-preview/))
- **Gemini Decision 1 (Google):** a small model that answers up to 1,000 yes-or-no or scoring questions in one pass, at $0.10 per million input tokens and free output. ([full article](/en/news/google-gemini-decisions-api-decision-1/))

### Anthropic products

Personal Finance in Claude, announced 5 November, is a US beta for Pro, Max and Ultra with read-only bank access through Plaid; Anthropic says Claude cannot move money. ([full article](/en/news/claude-personal-finance-beta/)) On 10 November Anthropic launched Claude Sheets and Whiteboards in beta on paid plans and made Dashboards generally available as Claude Data; Enterprise admins must switch on Sheets and Whiteboards, and Claude Data turns on by default on 19 November. ([full article](/en/news/claude-sheets-whiteboards-data/)) Claude Meetings, a beta for Team and Enterprise on Google Meet, takes notes and action items; notes are free during the beta. ([full article](/en/news/claude-meetings-beta/))

### OpenAI

OpenAI introduced Businesses in ChatGPT on 10 November: a beta Business Hub with product feeds and mention tracking, plus a Business Agent for eligible US ecommerce and online-service businesses that they can sponsor as a destination for ChatGPT ads. OpenAI cites 1.2 billion weekly users, its own figure. ([full article](/en/news/openai-businesses-in-chatgpt/)) Separately, Reuters, citing Bloomberg, reported that OpenAI closed $11 billion, the first part of a round valuing it at $1.25 trillion; OpenAI has not confirmed it and the reports name no investors. ([full article](/en/news/openai-1-25t-funding/))

### Security, chips and policy

- **Fireworks AI:** says an unauthorized party reached development machines on 24 October, affecting account and billing information and, for a limited set of accounts, secrets, datasets and incomplete models; it says card numbers were not affected. Users should check the bulletin and rotate any secrets stored there. ([full article](/en/news/fireworks-security-incident-update/))
- **Google TPUs:** TPU 8tx pairs the 8t chip with AMD hosts and hardware confidential compute; TPU 8i Max puts two 8i chips in one package. Google gave no absolute specifications or dates. ([full article](/en/news/google-tpu-8tx-8i-max/))
- **Policy:** President Trump posted on 8 November urging the Senate to pass S. 5653, which he says codifies a pledge that data centers pay for their own electricity. We read the post through an archive and could not check the bill text. ([full article](/en/news/trump-s5653-data-centers-pay-own-electricity/))

## Why it matters

Prices fell again at the top and the bottom of the market: Fable 5.2 is 20% cheaper than its predecessor, Hydrogen undercuts Argon, and Muse Ember and Decision 1 sit at ten cents per million input tokens. Most of the performance claims are the makers' own, so the numbers worth trusting this week are the prices and the dates.

The other thread is governance. Nvidia changed its chief executive with no stated reason and an independent investigation under way, and Anthropic's offering is moving toward a listing that is not yet effective. Both are situations where the next filing or statement matters more than any report about it.
