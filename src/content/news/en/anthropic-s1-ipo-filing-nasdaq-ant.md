## What happened

Anthropic, PBC, the company that makes Claude, filed a Form S-1 registration statement with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission on 26 October 2026 for a proposed initial public offering of its Class A common stock. In the filing, Anthropic says it has applied to list the shares on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the symbol "ANT". Its own announcement names Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley as sources of preliminary prospectus copies.

The filing leaves the number of shares and the price range blank, and Anthropic's announcement says the registration statement has not yet become effective and is not an offer to sell securities.

**Update, 4 November 2026:** on 2 November 2026 Anthropic filed Amendment No. 1 (Form S-1/A) to the registration statement. It adds results for the three and nine months ended 30 September 2026 and fills in the total of its compute and infrastructure commitments. The price range and share count are still blank in the amendment.

## Key details

The first group of figures below is from the original S-1, read on 26 October 2026. The group labelled Amendment No. 1 is from the S-1/A filed on 2 November, read on 4 November 2026. Dollar amounts are in millions as the filings present them.

- **Revenue:** $16,286 million for the six months ended 30 June 2026, against $1,163 million for the same period of 2025 (about 14 times higher). Full-year 2025 revenue was $4,585 million.
- **Losses:** net loss of $80,748 million for the first half of 2026 and $41,965 million for full-year 2025. Operating loss was $693 million in the first half of 2026 and $8,060 million in 2025. A footnote says changes in the fair value of convertible notes, derivative liabilities and SAFEs came to $72.4 billion in the first half of 2026, "primarily driven by an increase in the fair value of our outstanding convertible promissory notes", which accounts for most of the net loss.
- **Cash:** $131,651 million in cash, cash equivalents and short-term investments at 30 June 2026 ($112.4 billion in cash and equivalents plus $19.3 billion in short-term investments).
- **Customers:** more than 11,900 customers with at least $100,000 of trailing-twelve-month revenue as of 30 September 2026.
- **Structure:** Anthropic is a Delaware public benefit corporation. A single Class T share held by the Long-Term Benefit Trust lets the Trust elect a majority of the board; a Class F share issued to a founders' LLC carries 50.1% of the total voting power on matters put to stockholders generally. Anthropic says it will be a "controlled company" under Nasdaq rules, though it does not expect to rely on the exemption except for its nominating and corporate governance committee.
- **Use of proceeds:** "general corporate purposes", plus tax withholding and remittance obligations on employee equity.
- **Left blank in the original S-1:** the compute-commitment total was printed as "more than $ " with no figure in the version we read, and the filing summary gave no run-rate revenue number in the part we could read. We read the first 100,000 of the document's roughly 132,000 characters, so later sections may add detail.
- **Amendment No. 1, third quarter:** revenue of $16,956 million for the three months ended 30 September 2026 (the filing rounds this to $17.0 billion), up from $2,040 million in the fourth quarter of 2025 and $1,382 million in the third quarter of 2025. Operating loss was $567 million. Net loss was $23,291 million, of which $21,936 million is the change in fair value of convertible notes, derivative liabilities and SAFEs.
- **Amendment No. 1, nine months:** revenue of $33,242 million for the nine months ended 30 September 2026, against $2,545 million a year earlier. Operating loss was $1,260 million and net loss $104,039 million, with $94,324 million of the loss coming from the change in fair value of those instruments.
- **Amendment No. 1, cash:** $131,845 million in cash, cash equivalents and short-term investments at 30 September 2026, with an accumulated deficit of $156,193 million.
- **Amendment No. 1, commitments:** Anthropic says that as of and after 30 September 2026 it has entered into purchase commitments and lease agreements, including short-term commitments and leases not yet commenced, with an aggregate committed spend of approximately $895.4 billion, substantially all related to compute and infrastructure. About $447.5 billion of that is expected to be incurred before 31 December 2030. The filing says some of these agreements include minimum purchase and take-or-pay terms.
- **Still blank in the amendment:** the price range and share count, and the run-rate revenue figure as of 31 October 2026.
- **Reported, not in the filing text we read:** Forbes and PPC Land report a valuation range of $1.5 trillion to $2 trillion, citing press reports, and marketing the week of 9 November with a listing the week of 16 November. Anthropic has not confirmed either in the materials we read.

## Why it matters

The S-1 is the first audited look at the finances of a company whose models many readers use daily. For the AI tools market, it puts hard numbers on how fast one frontier lab's revenue grew and how much of its reported loss comes from accounting for its own rising valuation rather than operations. It also shows the governance model, a public benefit corporation steered by a trust, that investors would be buying into.

For people who build on Claude, a public listing means regular financial disclosure, and risk factors the company must publish, including a line in its summary that its "AI models may behave in unintended, misaligned, or autonomous ways." Nothing in the filing changes Claude's products or prices. Terms can still change before the offering becomes effective, so treat the IPO timing as unconfirmed until Anthropic sets a price range.

The amendment adds one thing the first filing did not show: the size of the obligations behind that growth. A committed spend of about $895.4 billion is large compared with the $33.2 billion of revenue reported for nine months. The filing says some of the underlying agreements carry minimum purchase or take-or-pay terms and that about half is expected to be incurred before the end of 2030, so how much is fixed and when it falls due is something readers of the filing will want to check.
