## What happened

On 13 November 2026 TechCrunch published a report on new research by Will DePue of Unemployment Labs, a former OpenAI researcher. According to TechCrunch, DePue analyzed the weights of open models from DeepSeek, Moonshot, MiniMax and Ant Group and says he has "convincing evidence" that Kimi K3 and DeepSeek V4 carry "fingerprints" of training on Nvidia's Blackwell chips. We read the TechCrunch article on 14 November 2026. The analysis itself is not linked there, so we have not seen it, and everything below is attributed to TechCrunch.

## Key details

- **The claim, as reported:** the evidence is in the model weights, and TechCrunch attributes the finding to DePue. TechCrunch also says the evidence does not show that the models were trained entirely on Nvidia hardware or that any law was broken, because the chips could sit in data centers outside China.
- **Government statement, as reported:** TechCrunch says White House technology adviser Michael Kratsios said earlier this year that Moonshot used banned chips, citing US intelligence sources. We have not seen a primary record of that statement.
- **Chip flows, from Epoch AI as cited by TechCrunch:** about 2 million advanced chips entered China in 2024 and 2025, and 275,000 Nvidia accelerators were imported into Malaysia over the same period.
- **Not available:** a link to the DePue analysis, a citable White House statement, and responses from Moonshot, DeepSeek, MiniMax, Ant Group or Nvidia.

## Why it matters

Export controls on advanced chips are a central issue in AI policy, and a method for inferring training hardware from released weights would be new evidence in that debate. For now it is one researcher's claim relayed by one outlet, with no company response and no primary document we can check.

Readers should treat it as unconfirmed. Related reporting on Nvidia's rules for overseas cloud partners, also unconfirmed, is covered in our earlier article on the reported 51% requirement.
