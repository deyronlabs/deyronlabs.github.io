## What happened

On 13 November 2026 (17:00 UTC) Google published a post saying Google Wallet is now a connected app in the Gemini app's Personal Intelligence settings. Google says it is "rolling out today for all Gemini app users in the U.S.", on web and on mobile (Android and iOS). Users tag @Google Wallet in a Gemini chat to get answers informed by their own spending.

## Key details

Everything below is from Google's post, read on 14 November 2026. We have not tested the feature.

- **Examples Google gives:** charts of cash flow, asset allocation and budgets generated in the chat; finding forgotten or recurring subscriptions and price increases; building a holiday budget from last year's gift and travel spending.
- **Optional bank link:** users can link credit card and bank accounts to Google Wallet through Plaid for what Google calls deeper insights.
- **Controls:** the connection is opt-in, set under "Connected Apps" in Personal Intelligence settings, and can be switched off at any time. Google says Gemini "can only read information from your accounts" and cannot move, manage or invest money. A Memory Summary page lets users view, edit or remove saved details.
- **Availability:** the United States only. The post does not name a plan tier or list languages.
- **Not stated in the post:** any usage numbers, error rates, how long data is kept, or a date for other countries.

## Why it matters

This puts Gemini closer to a personal finance assistant that works from real transaction data, which is more useful than generic budgeting advice but also a larger privacy decision. The read-only limit is the key design choice: the assistant can analyse spending but, by Google's description, cannot act on it.

For users, the practical questions are what exactly Gemini stores in memory and whether the Plaid link is worth it. Google's help page linked from the post is the place to check before connecting accounts. Gemini answers about money should be checked against the actual statements.
