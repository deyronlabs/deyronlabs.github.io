## What happened

On 17 November 2026 NVIDIA released results for the third quarter of fiscal 2027, which ended on 25 October 2026. It is the first quarterly release since Jensen Huang's resignation on 12 November; the release is quoted from "Colette Kress, interim CEO and executive vice president and chief financial officer" and refers to "NVIDIA's leadership transition and the search for a new chief executive officer". It does not mention Huang by name. All figures below are from NVIDIA's release and the matching SEC filing.

## Key details

- **Revenue:** $115.8 billion, up 20% from $96.2 billion in the second quarter and up 103% from $57.0 billion a year earlier.
- **Segments:** Data Center $107.6 billion (up 21% quarter on quarter, 110% year on year); Edge Computing $8.2 billion (up 14% and 42%).
- **Profit:** GAAP gross margin 74.8% (non-GAAP 74.9%); GAAP net income $65.3 billion; GAAP diluted EPS $2.70, non-GAAP $2.68.
- **Outlook:** Q4 revenue of $130.0 billion, plus or minus 3%, in a quarter with an extra week, with no Data Center compute revenue from China assumed. Gross margin guided to 71.0% to 72.0%.
- **Capital return:** a $0.25 per share quarterly dividend payable on 23 December 2026 to holders of record on 2 December; $28.1 billion returned in the quarter through repurchases and dividends; about $227.3 billion left on the buyback authorization after a $150 billion increase approved in September.
- **Products:** NVIDIA says it "ramped production and began shipping NVIDIA Vera Rubin NVL72", calling it the fastest product ramp in its history. Kress is quoted: "Vera Rubin is off to an explosive start, our roadmap is on track." The release also says Anthropic's access to Grace Blackwell and Vera Rubin capacity was expanded.
- **Balance sheet (SEC filing):** cash and equivalents $29.1 billion and long-term debt $32.4 billion at 25 October 2026.
- **Not stated:** any detail on the CEO search, and any comment on Huang's departure.

## Why it matters

For anyone tracking AI infrastructure, the numbers show demand for NVIDIA hardware still growing at roughly the same pace as a year ago, and the Q4 guide shows NVIDIA planning without China data center compute. The results are NVIDIA's own, issued during an unusual leadership transition, so the open questions are who becomes permanent CEO and whether the Vera Rubin ramp holds as described.
