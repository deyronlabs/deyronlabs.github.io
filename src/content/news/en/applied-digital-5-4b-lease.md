## What happened

Reuters reported on 23 November 2026 that Applied Digital, a company that builds and operates data centers for AI workloads, signed a lease with an investment-grade hyperscaler that Reuters says was not named. We read the Reuters report; it is the only source we could read. Applied Digital's own announcement and any SEC filing were not readable to us, so everything below is Reuters' account of the deal.

## Key details

- **Capacity and value:** 150 MW of IT capacity, with $5.4 billion of contracted revenue over an 18-year base term, per Reuters.
- **Extension options:** the term can be extended to 33 years, which Reuters puts at about $12.7 billion of revenue if all options are exercised.
- **Earlier lease:** Reuters notes a previous lease from June of 210 MW over 15 years for about $5.2 billion, at the Delta Forge 2 site.
- **Backlog:** Reuters cites more than $41 billion of total contracted revenue, about $100 billion including renewals, with roughly 75% from investment-grade tenants.
- **Financing:** a $5 billion partnership with Macquarie plus project financing, per Reuters, which quotes the company's CFO, Mohmand.
- **Timing:** delivery from the first half of 2028, subject to conditions.
- **Not confirmed here:** the tenant's name, the site, power terms, and the company's own wording. We did not read the release or any 8-K.

## Why it matters

Long leases like this show how hyperscalers are locking up AI data center capacity years ahead of when it is built, and how developers such as Applied Digital finance that capacity with contracted revenue as collateral. An 18-year base term with delivery in 2028 means this capacity is a bet on demand well beyond the current model generation.

Because the tenant is unnamed and the report is the only source we read, treat the figures as reported, not confirmed. Watch for the company's own filing, which should state the tenant relationship, site and conditions.
