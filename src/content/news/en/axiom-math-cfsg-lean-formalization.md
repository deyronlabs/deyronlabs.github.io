## What happened

Axiom Math has published a project page for a Lean formalization of the first-generation proof of the classification of finite simple groups (CFSG), a result the page describes as spanning thousands of journal pages written by about a hundred mathematicians. The page is undated; aggregator coverage placed the announcement on 27 October 2026.

## Key details

- **Size (Axiom's figures):** 43.1 million lines of Lean and 2.05 million declarations.
- **Verification claim:** the page states that every logical step is checked by Lean's kernel. We have not independently run or audited the code.
- **Deviation from the literature:** the authors say they found an issue in a published argument for the Unbalanced Group Theorem and used an alternative route, which an accompanying paper explains. Some secondary coverage cites larger numbers of deviations and a 24-day build time; we could not find either on the project page, so we do not report them.
- **People:** the page names Dean Cureton, Vasily Ilin, Bhavik Mehta and Niels Voss as core contributors and Letong Hong as principal investigator.
- **Page inconsistencies:** the page lists 100 milestones in one place and 108 in another, and the source counts differ between sections, so exact totals should be treated as approximate.

## Why it matters

Machine-checked proofs of very large human arguments are one of the clearest tests of AI-assisted mathematics, because a proof checker, not the model, decides whether it is correct. If the formalization stands, it would make a famously long and hard-to-audit proof checkable end to end. For now this is Axiom's own presentation: the date, build process and the extent of AI involvement are not documented on the page, and mathematicians have not yet reviewed whether the Lean statements faithfully capture the theorem.
