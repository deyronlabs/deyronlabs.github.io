## What happened

On 8 October 2026, Anthropic announced the commitment at the Science: A New Golden Age Summit in Washington, DC, hosted by the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy. It describes the Genesis Mission as a federal initiative to accelerate scientific and technological discovery with AI, and says it first announced its partnership with the Department of Energy on the initiative in December.

## Key details

All figures below are Anthropic's own.

- **Amount:** $150 million over three years.
- **Agencies:** the goal is to make Claude available to more than 15 Genesis Mission agencies, including NASA, the National Institutes of Health and the National Science Foundation.
- **Research projects:** Claude, Claude Code and API credits for several hundred Genesis Mission research projects.
- **Priorities:** work with agencies and national laboratories on top science priorities, including fusion energy and quantum computing, plus training, onboarding and technical support for scientists.
- **Related programs Anthropic mentions:** Claude Science, an AI workbench launched earlier in 2026; 10,000 free and discounted seats for academic scientists; an expanded AI for Science program with credits; and a research preview of a Model Hardware Standard for AI agents to operate lab instruments safely.
- **Not in the post:** how the $150 million is split between credits, staff and other support, and any measurable research goals.

## Why it matters

The announcement is mainly about government and research access to Anthropic's models rather than a new product. For scientists at participating agencies and labs, it signals free or subsidized access to Claude and Claude Code.

It also shows how AI labs compete for public-sector science work. Because the commitment is described largely in credits and support, its size in cash terms is unclear, and the post does not say how results will be measured.
