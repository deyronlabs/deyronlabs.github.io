## What happened

On 27 October 2026 Anthropic announced an expanded partnership with the US General Services Administration (GSA). Under the agreement, as Anthropic describes it, government partners get a 40% discount on Claude Enterprise, Claude for Government and the Claude API. The discount covers every product surface, including direct purchases and, "in the coming months", purchases through cloud providers, and it now extends to state, local, tribal and territorial governments. Agencies can buy at the discounted rate through GSA's Multiple Award Schedule.

## Key details

- **Scale (per Anthropic):** approximately 500 US federal, state and local government organizations now use Claude. Weekly Claude for Government active users are up about eightfold since February, and usage across federal civilian agencies has grown about 20-fold in a year.
- **Savings:** Anthropic estimates the discounts offered through the agreement could save the government $1 billion. Agencies are not bound by a minimum spend commitment.
- **Security status:** Claude for Government became generally available on 30 September and offers near-feature parity with commercial products at FedRAMP High. Anthropic says Claude on Amazon Bedrock is FedRAMP High, with select models authorized for DoD IL4 and IL5 in AWS GovCloud, and that Claude on Vertex AI is FedRAMP High. Claude Enterprise is "in process" for FedRAMP Moderate under the FedRAMP 20x program.
- **Examples Anthropic cites:** a Utah pilot gave Claude Code to 34 developers across 10 state agencies; 71% reported saving 20 or more hours over four weeks. A state developer moved nearly 900 API methods to a new framework in two days on a project scoped for a month. These are customer accounts reported by Anthropic.
- **Next step:** Anthropic says a dedicated agency-implementation program is coming "in the coming weeks".

## Why it matters

A 40% list-price cut for a whole customer class, with no minimum spend, lowers the cost of trying Claude for thousands of public bodies and puts pricing pressure on rivals that sell to government. The usage figures show how quickly public-sector adoption is moving, but they are Anthropic's numbers, and the $1 billion figure is an estimate rather than committed spending. The announcement also concerns a company that this publication uses as a tool, so readers should weigh it as Anthropic's own account of its business.
