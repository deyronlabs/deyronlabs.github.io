## What happened

On 20 October 2026 Perplexity announced that **Computer**, its agent that carries out tasks on a user's behalf, can now run fully on-device on a Mac. According to Perplexity, "Running Computer locally starts on the Mac, keeping the task's planning, reasoning, and file processing on device."

## Key details

- **Models:** the local agent runs Qwen 3.6 or PPLX Pro, which Perplexity describes as a post-trained version of Qwen. If the user authorises cloud escalation, the local orchestrator can call one of 15+ frontier models for harder reasoning.
- **Hardware:** Apple silicon Macs. Perplexity cites the new Mac mini with M6 and M5 Pro as an example and recommends 36 GB of memory or more.
- **Availability:** Perplexity Pro, Max and Enterprise subscribers, inside the Perplexity Mac app. The page gives no price. Setup is: install the app, download a local model, then choose "fully local" for a task.
- **Apps mentioned:** Notes, Messages, Mail and Numbers.
- **Related, same day:** Perplexity also said Computer can be deployed on institutions' own servers, including air-gapped networks, with UK sovereign-infrastructure company Valarian. Deployments are "available starting today" by contacting Perplexity; no pricing is given.
- **Not independently verified:** the privacy and on-device claims come from Perplexity's own description, and the page publishes no performance or accuracy figures for the local models.

## Why it matters

Running an agent that reads mail and messages entirely on the laptop is a meaningful privacy option for people who do not want that data in the cloud, and it shows open-weight Qwen models being used inside a mainstream Western product. The trade-off is hardware: the 36 GB recommendation excludes many Macs, and without published benchmarks it is unclear how the local models compare with the cloud ones Perplexity routes to.
