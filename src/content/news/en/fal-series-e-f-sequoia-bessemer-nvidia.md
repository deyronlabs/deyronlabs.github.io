## What happened

fal, a San Francisco-based company that hosts and runs generative media models, announced two funding rounds together in early November 2026. In its own blog post it says the Series E was led by Sequoia Capital and closed earlier this year, and the Series F was led by Bessemer Venture Partners with continued investment from NVIDIA.

fal's post does not give any amounts or a valuation. The numbers in this article come from press reports and are marked as reported.

## Key details

What fal confirms is limited to the post, read on 4 November 2026. The financial figures below are from TechCrunch and Wowtale, which we could not match against a company statement.

- **Confirmed by fal:** a Series E led by Sequoia Capital, and a Series F led by Bessemer Venture Partners with continued investment from NVIDIA. fal says the funds are for inference capacity and for its team, and will support work across its ecosystem in 2027.
- **Reported amount:** TechCrunch reports the latest round raised $1.3 billion, with Sound Ventures and T. Rowe Price also participating, and that $1.65 billion was raised across the two rounds. Wowtale notes the figures imply about $350 million for the Series E; that is its own arithmetic, not a stated figure.
- **Reported valuation:** TechCrunch, citing Bloomberg, says the round values fal at $16 billion, double its $8 billion valuation in April. We did not read Bloomberg's report, which is behind a paywall.
- **Reported revenue:** TechCrunch, again citing Bloomberg, says fal passed $800 million in annualized revenue earlier this year. fal has not published this figure in the post we read.
- **What fal does (company description, via Wowtale):** customers call image, video, audio and 3D models through an API without provisioning GPUs; fal says it hosts more than 1,000 models.

## Why it matters

For developers and companies building with generative media models, the confirmed part is that fal says it has new funding aimed at inference capacity, which is the compute needed to run models for customers. Nothing in fal's post changes pricing, supported models or terms.

The reported numbers put fal among several inference providers that have raised large rounds this year. TechCrunch, citing people familiar with the companies' financials, says those providers' gross margins remain thin because of the cost of buying or leasing compute. Treat the amounts and valuation as unconfirmed until fal or its investors publish them.
