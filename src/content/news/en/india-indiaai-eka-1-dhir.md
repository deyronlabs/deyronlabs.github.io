## What happened

India's Press Information Bureau published a release on 3 November 2026 saying the IndiaAI Mission, run by the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology, launched three foundation models in New Delhi: EKA-1, built by Soket Labs, and Dhīr 2B and Dhīr 5B, built by ZenteiQ. The announcement reached our feed on 13 November, ten days after the release. This article relies only on the government's release, so every claim below is the government's, not independently verified.

## Key details

- **EKA-1 (Soket Labs):** the release describes an open, sparse hybrid language model with 26.3 billion parameters, trained on about 7.5 trillion tokens. It is said to cover all 22 scheduled Indian languages plus English and to target mathematics, code, reasoning and agentic use.
- **Dhīr 2B and Dhīr 5B (ZenteiQ):** described as open-source models for English and 10 Indian languages, designed to run on-premises and offline for defence, government, industry and education.
- **Compute:** the release says the models were built with subsidized national compute under the IndiaAI Mission's foundation model program.
- **Access:** the release says the models "can be made accessible" through AIKosh, the government's AI platform.
- **Not stated in the release:** the exact license, download links, benchmark results, the full Dhīr specifications and the program's cost.

## Why it matters

Models tuned for Indian languages and meant to run offline address a gap that general-purpose systems cover unevenly. Without benchmarks, a license or download links, though, it is not yet possible to judge quality or whether developers can build on them. Watch for the AIKosh listings and model cards, which would show the terms and the first measurable results.
