## What happened

On 1 October 2026, arXiv published "Fair Moderation, Equitable Access, and AI: arXiv's Updated Rate Limit Policy". It says arXiv "now limits submitters to up to two submissions per calendar month". The policy took effect the same day and applies to all categories.

## Key details

- **Limits:** two submissions per calendar month, and three active submissions at any time (the active-submission cap has been in place since 2024).
- **Counting rules:** rejected submissions count toward the monthly limit. Submissions on hold from a prior month do not count against the next month's limit, but do count toward the active limit. Papers deleted before announcement do not count toward either. Co-authors are not counted against a submitter's limit.
- **Reason given:** arXiv says a sharp rise in submissions is overwhelming its volunteer moderators. It reports 40,363 submissions in September 2026, compared with 20,569 in September 2024. It cites thin papers, "salami" papers that split one result into several, and dense AI-written papers, and says "a relatively small proportion of authors are submitting a large number of low-quality papers".
- **Status:** arXiv calls the policy a "stopgap" while it works out best practices for AI-assisted authors, and says it will monitor the effects and adjust. The post describes no process for requesting a higher limit.

## Why it matters

arXiv is where much AI research first appears, so a cap on submissions affects how fast new results reach readers. Authors with a backlog or a conference deadline are asked to plan around the limits.

The policy shows one effect of cheap text generation: platforms that rely on volunteer review are adding rate limits. Whether a flat cap catches low-quality submissions without slowing legitimate high-output authors is something arXiv says it will monitor.
