## What happened

On 10 November 2026 Tom's Hardware published an analysis saying that Nvidia has told cloud partners around the world that at least 51% of their services must go to non-Chinese companies. The piece names the Financial Times as the original source for that rule. We read the Tom's Hardware article on 10 November 2026, but not the FT report, which is behind a paywall. Nvidia has not published the rule, and Tom's Hardware writes that Nvidia "hasn't confirmed or denied" it. Everything below is attributed to Tom's Hardware and, where stated, the FT.

## Key details

- **The rule, as reported:** the instruction was given verbally through Nvidia's sales and regional teams and applies globally. Existing contracts reportedly stay as they are, while new ones would need to follow the 51% requirement.
- **Tracking:** Tom's Hardware says Nvidia has asked Southeast Asian providers that want to be Nvidia Cloud Partners to use tracking called "Fleet Intelligence", and that an approved-buyer list has cut the number of authorized buyers in Asia to less than half.
- **Prices:** Tom's Hardware reports that a B300 rack that costs about $600,000 internationally sold for about $2.8 million in China last month.
- **Demand:** it also reports Chinese orders for the RTX Pro 5500 of more than 6 million cards, against about 3 million that could be produced in 2027.
- **Legislation:** the article notes that the Remote Access Security Act has passed the House and has not been voted on in the Senate. It also says Nvidia reports third-quarter results on 17 November.
- **Not known:** whether Nvidia will confirm the rule, the text of the FT report, when exactly the rule applies, and any official wording.

## Why it matters

If accurate, the rule would reach beyond chip sales to how cloud providers outside China rent out Nvidia GPUs, which affects where AI compute capacity is available and to whom. Because Nvidia has not confirmed it and the instruction was reportedly verbal, details such as how compliance would be measured are unknown. Readers should treat the figures as reported claims. Nvidia's 17 November results are one place the company might address the matter.
