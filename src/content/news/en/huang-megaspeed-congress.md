## What happened

On 5 November 2026 Axios reported that three Republican chairs of House committees, Brian J. Mast, John Moolenaar and Rick Crawford, sent Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang a letter asking for a closed-door briefing. The chairs lead the Foreign Affairs Committee, the Select Committee on the CCP and the Select Committee on Intelligence. According to Axios, they want to hear about Nvidia's protocols for protecting against foreign adversaries and about the company's interactions with Alice Huang. We read the Axios article on 9 November 2026. The allegations about Alice Huang come from press reports that Axios cites, not from a court or a government finding, and we have not confirmed them.

## Update, 11 November 2026

Nvidia's board has since announced its own review. In a statement dated 8 November 2026, the board said it is starting an independent investigation "in light of recent reporting that founder and CEO Jensen Huang has been the target of a foreign intelligence effort", and that it "has no reason to believe that either national security or NVIDIA secrets were compromised". In a one-paragraph statement dated 10 November 2026, the independent members of the board said they have retained Joon H. Kim, a former acting U.S. Attorney for the Southern District of New York, and his firm Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz to conduct a thorough and independent investigation. We read both statements on Nvidia's newsroom. The 10 November statement gives no deadline, scope or findings, and we did not read Nvidia's internal message to employees of 8 November. The text we read does not mention the House request, so we do not link the two beyond the dates.

## Key details

- **The request:** a briefing at closed doors, asked for in a letter Axios says was sent on Thursday, 5 November 2026. Axios does not report a hearing date.
- **The reports behind it:** Axios cites The New York Times, which reported a close relationship and said intelligence officials had briefed President Trump, and The Wall Street Journal, which reported that officials from at least three countries had concerns that Alice Huang passes information to the Chinese government, possibly including about Nvidia chips. Axios describes these claims as alleged or reported.
- **Nvidia's response:** per Axios, Nvidia says meetings with customers are part of the job and that regional cloud customers "receive ordinary business information — not sensitive NVIDIA trade secrets."
- **China's response:** per Axios, the Chinese government called the reports "groundless speculation."
- **Board review:** the board announced an independent investigation on 8 November and retained outside counsel on 10 November; see the update above. Nvidia has not published findings.
- **Not stated:** the Axios page does not describe a new chip export rule or license, and it does not report evidence made public. We did not find the company Megaspeed mentioned in the text we read, so we do not connect it to this story.

## Why it matters

The request shows congressional interest in how Nvidia, whose chips supply much of the AI industry, protects information when it deals with customers in China and elsewhere. For now it is a request for a briefing plus a board-led investigation with no published findings, with no announced hearing, rule or conclusion, and the underlying allegations are unproven and denied. Users and developers are not affected directly. The practical thing to watch is whether the letter leads to formal action on chip export oversight, which would be reported by the committees or by Nvidia.
