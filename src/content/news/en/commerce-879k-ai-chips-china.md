## What happened

CNBC reported on 19 November 2026 that documents published by the House Foreign Affairs Committee show the Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) approved sales of more than 879,000 AI chips to Chinese companies through May 2026. The figures are the committee's, as CNBC reports them. We read the CNBC article in full, but we have not read the committee documents themselves, so every number below is attributed to that reporting.

## Key details

- **Approvals:** more than 879,000 AI chips approved for Chinese companies through May 2026. CNBC names Alibaba and Tencent among the recipients.
- **Requests:** about 3.4 million requests between December and May, from Nvidia and AMD, according to the documents as CNBC describes them. Bernstein analyst Rasgon is quoted as estimating that roughly one in four requests was approved; that is his estimate, and it is consistent with the two figures above.
- **Pentagon-listed firms:** CNBC reports that some approvals went to companies the Defense Department designates as Chinese military companies.
- **Allegation by a lawmaker:** Representative Mast said two applicants, Maginfra and New Matrix, were fake companies. This is his claim as reported by CNBC; the article does not report a response from either company.
- **Company responses:** Nvidia said it complied with regulations and has stopped shipping H200 chips to China. AMD said it complies with the law. BIS said licensing is decided through an interagency process, with strict conditions and limited volumes.
- **Context from CNBC:** Nvidia reported $2.4 billion of H200 sales to China in its latest quarter. We covered Nvidia's statement on halting licensed Hopper shipments to China in an earlier article.
- **Not in the reporting we read:** the committee documents themselves, the breakdown by company, and how many of the approved chips were shipped.

## Why it matters

The figures give the first public sense of scale for US licensing of AI chips to China, which until now has mostly been discussed through individual company statements. For anyone following export controls, the open questions are how many approved chips actually shipped, to whom, and whether the conditions BIS describes were enforced. Those details are in the underlying documents, which should be read before drawing conclusions. The number is also not a measure of what Chinese labs can train today, since it covers approvals, not deliveries.
