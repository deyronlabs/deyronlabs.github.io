## What happened

On 16 November 2026 the Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS), part of the US Department of Commerce, announced an interim final rule on remote access to advanced "super intelligence" (SI) chips and servers. BIS says the rule was written by and at the direction of the Department of the Treasury and the National Security Council. The release was read in BIS's news listing on 17 November; we did not read the rule text itself.

## Key details

- **Stated purpose (BIS):** to ensure that existing restrictions on access to sensitive US technology cannot be bypassed through remote-compute arrangements with data centers in third countries.
- **Approach (BIS):** the rule "aligns protections" for remote access with the existing controls on physical shipments.
- **Status:** BIS says the rule is on public display. The Federal Register lists it as "Addressing Remote Access to Advanced Semiconductors" (document 2026-23529), scheduled for publication on 19 November 2026. The rule text is available there only as an unpublished PDF, which we did not open.
- **Terminology:** "super intelligence" is BIS's wording in the release; we quote it as used.
- **Not stated in the release:** how "SI chips or servers" are defined, any performance thresholds, which countries or entities are covered, who needs a license, the effective date, and the comment period.

## Why it matters

If the rule works as BIS describes, renting compute abroad would no longer sidestep controls that apply to shipping chips, which is most relevant to cloud and data-center operators and to companies that train models on overseas capacity. For people using AI tools, there is no stated effect. Because the release gives no thresholds or country lists, the practical reach of the rule is unknown until the full text is published, and we plan to update this article then.
