## What happened

On 23 October 2026 the Texas Attorney General's office announced that Ken Paxton had sued OpenAI and its CEO, Sam Altman. The press release names OpenAI Global, LLC and affiliated entities, together with Altman, and says the suit is brought under the Texas Deceptive Trade Practices–Consumer Protection Act. The press release says the state alleges OpenAI marketed ChatGPT as safe and reliable while failing to protect Texans, including children, from harms linked to relying on the chatbot.

These are the state's allegations in a newly filed complaint. No court has ruled on them, and this article could not read the complaint itself. OpenAI's reply, as reported by the Texas Tribune and cited by Dr. Web, points to its own safety measures and says governments should help set strong AI safety standards; no formal court response has been reported.

## Key details

Everything below is attributed to the Texas Attorney General's release unless stated otherwise.

- **Claims described in the release:** that language models can produce inaccurate or harmful output without adequate safeguards and that users are not told of this risk; that OpenAI misrepresented its products; and that it was driven by competition and profit. The release lists alleged harms including aiding mass shooters, encouraging suicide, public humiliation of professionals, loss of critical thinking, and minors becoming addicted while their data was collected without parental oversight.
- **Quote:** Paxton said, "I will fight to protect Texans from the harms of AI and ensure that powerful technology companies are not above the law."
- **Court, additional claims and relief (secondary report):** Dr. Web, summarizing the complaint, reports that it was filed in Collin County district court, also cites the state's SCOPE Act on minors, and seeks civil penalties of up to $10,000 per violation, disgorgement of profits, an order barring ChatGPT for minors in Texas, court-appointed independent review before new models reach Texas users, and a jury trial. The press release as read here does not state these terms, so treat them as unconfirmed until the complaint is checked.
- **Status:** complaint stage only. No findings have been made.

## Why it matters

For people who use ChatGPT, nothing changes today: the service is unchanged and the claims are untested. What the case shows is that state attorneys general are testing consumer-protection law against AI chatbot makers, and, in this suit, against a chief executive personally. If the relief described by secondary reports were ever ordered, it could mean age limits or pre-release review for AI products in one US state, which would affect every company that offers chatbots to minors, not only OpenAI.

The outcome depends on what the complaint actually alleges and on OpenAI's defense, neither of which is yet public in full. We will update this article if the complaint or a court response becomes available.
