## What happened

On 18 November 2026 Palo Alto Networks (NASDAQ: PANW) announced that it has entered into a definitive agreement to acquire Applied Compute, which the company describes as a platform for post-training and inference of open-weight AI models. According to the press release, the transaction is all stock and is valued at $4.5 billion, subject to adjustments. We read the release on the company's investor site on 19 November 2026.

## Key details

- **Price and structure:** all-stock, valued at $4.5 billion "subject to adjustments", according to Palo Alto Networks. The release gives no price per share and does not describe the adjustments.
- **Timing:** closing is subject to customary conditions, including regulatory approval, and is expected in Palo Alto Networks' fiscal third quarter of 2027.
- **People:** Yash Patil, co-founder and CEO of Applied Compute, becomes Co-Chief AI Officer of Palo Alto Networks after closing, and Applied Compute's research team joins the company.
- **Customers:** Applied Compute will keep serving AI-native companies, digital natives and large enterprises, and Palo Alto Networks plans to bring the platform to its own customers.
- **Company statements:** Chairman and CEO Nikesh Arora is quoted calling it the "best post-training and inference platform we've seen". The release also says Palo Alto's Unit 42 Frontier AI Assessment confirmed Applied Compute's security foundations. Both are claims by the buyer in its own release.
- **Reported, not in the release:** AI Weekly, citing the Wall Street Journal, reports a price about $1.25 billion above the $3.25 billion post-money valuation of Applied Compute's $350 million round of 2 November, and a rise of about 8% in Palo Alto shares after the announcement. We have not verified either figure against a primary source.
- **Not stated:** the release gives no revenue or customer numbers for Applied Compute and no timeline for integrating the product. Palo Alto Networks said more detail would come on its fiscal first-quarter 2027 earnings call on 18 November.

## Why it matters

The deal puts a platform for tuning and serving open-weight models inside one of the largest security vendors, which signals that the company wants to offer enterprises a route to running their own models rather than only protecting other people's. How much that changes for customers is still unknown, because the release says nothing about pricing, product plans or integration, and the deal has not closed or cleared regulators. Anyone relying on Applied Compute today should watch for the integration details promised after the earnings call.
