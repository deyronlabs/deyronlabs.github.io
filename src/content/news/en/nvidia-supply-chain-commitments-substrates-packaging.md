## What happened

On 17 November 2026 NVIDIA announced expanded commitments with supply chain partners "to support AI infrastructure buildout". The partners named are Amkor Technology, Foxconn, Kinsus Interconnect Technology, Samsung Electro-Mechanics, SPIL and TSMC. All figures below are from NVIDIA's announcement and are NVIDIA's own claims; the partners' terms were not independently checked.

## Key details

- **Kinsus:** a new agreement to expand ABF substrate capacity dedicated to NVIDIA "by more than 3.5x over current levels" at its Youth campus in Taoyuan, Taiwan. Production is expected to begin in the second half of 2027, with the added capacity targeted for the third quarter of 2028.
- **Samsung Electro-Mechanics:** an amended substrate supply agreement to improve operations at existing factories.
- **Amkor:** multiyear agreements announced in July and October 2026 total "nearly $3 billion", including prepayments.
- **SPIL:** a multiyear expansion in Taiwan "supported by multibillion-dollar funding from NVIDIA", increasing contracted advanced packaging capacity by mid-2029.
- **Products:** NVIDIA says Vera Rubin NVL72 and Vera CPUs had their first volume shipment in the third quarter, built on TSMC 5nm, 3nm and CoWoS.
- **Context:** NVIDIA says the update builds on October 2026 commitments that it described as a 2.5x substrate capacity increase over three years.
- **Quote:** Debora Shoquist, NVIDIA's executive vice president of operations: "Our supply chain is a strategic advantage."
- **Not stated:** NVIDIA's own spending totals for the Kinsus, Samsung Electro-Mechanics or SPIL agreements, and prices.

## Why it matters

Substrates and advanced packaging are physical limits on how many AI accelerators can ship, so dated capacity targets show where NVIDIA expects supply to tighten and when it expects relief: new Kinsus output only starts in the second half of 2027. The dates and dollar figures are NVIDIA's; delivery against them will be visible only in later shipments and results.
