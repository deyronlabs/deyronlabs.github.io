## What you will learn

You will create an image from a text description, change it by describing the change, edit one of your own photos, and avoid the four mistakes that waste most people's time. No Photoshop, no layers, no masks. All you need is a free Google account.

## What you need

- A Google account.
- The Gemini app, which opens at [gemini.google.com](https://gemini.google.com).

Everything in this guide works on the free plan. Usage limits can change at any time, because Google sets them.

**A note on versions.** Google released Nano Banana 2.1 on 6 October 2026. When this episode was recorded, a free account still showed Nano Banana 2 under the response details, while 2.1 was available in Google AI Studio on a paid plan or with a paid API key. The steps are the same for both. If your results look a little different from the ones in the video, that is the reason.

## Step 1: Create an image

Open a new chat in Gemini. Click the plus button in the message box and choose **Create image**. You will see a row of style templates and a box that says "Describe your image". The templates are fun, but start from a plain description first.

## Step 2: Write your first prompt

Give the model enough to work with. A simple formula: say what the subject is, where it is, what the light is like, and what kind of picture you want. The prompt used in the episode:

> A product photo of a ceramic coffee mug with the word LAB printed on it, on a wooden desk next to a green plant, soft morning light.

The exact word is in capital letters on purpose. Short words like this usually come out right. Press enter and wait about ten to twenty seconds. If you do not like the result, press the regenerate button (the circular arrow under the image) for a different version. If it is close but not right, do not start over: edit it.

## Step 3: Choose the aspect ratio first

Under the message box, the **Aspect ratio** menu lets you pick the shape before you generate:

- Square.
- 9:16, for vertical stories and Shorts.
- 3:4 and 4:3.
- 16:9, for video thumbnails and slides.

Pick the shape you actually need now. Changing it later usually means generating again.

## Step 4: Edit by asking

This is what makes the tool different: you edit the image by talking to it. In the same chat, the episode used this prompt:

> Edit this image: make the mug matte dark green, with the text LAB in white. Keep everything else exactly the same.

The mug and the text changed color, and the desk, the plant, the light and the notebook stayed where they were. The sentence **"Keep everything else exactly the same"** does real work. Always include it.

## Step 5: Make several changes in one message

You can ask for more than one change at a time:

> Remove the notebook from the desk, and add a small paper airplane next to the mug. Keep the lighting and everything else the same.

If the model changes something you did not ask for, say so in the next message, for example: "The plant changed. Put it back as in the previous version." It usually listens.

## Step 6: Edit your own photo

Click the plus button again, choose **Upload files**, select a picture, and describe the change. Examples:

- Remove the person in the background.
- Make the sky look like sunset.
- Change the red jacket to blue.

**Privacy first.** Google says on the Gemini home screen that human reviewers may look at some saved chats to improve its AI. You can change this in Activity settings, or use a temporary chat. Do not upload documents, photos of other people's faces, or anything private that you would not want a stranger to see.

## Four mistakes to avoid

1. **Small text.** Asked to add a small line reading "Deyron Labs, image editing test, 2026", the model added it, and it looked right at first glance. Small letters are the weak spot. Google itself lists small text as a known limitation, so zoom in and check every character before you publish.
2. **Vague requests.** "Make it better" tells the model nothing. Say exactly what should change.
3. **Too many changes at once.** Three or four changes per message is fine. Ten is how things start to drift.
4. **Forgetting it is an AI image.** If you publish it, say so. YouTube asks you to label realistic AI content, and it is the honest thing to do everywhere else too.

## Download your image

When you are happy with the result, click the download icon under the image. The file is yours to use, but check Google's terms for commercial use before you build a product on top of it.

## About Nano Banana 2.1

According to Google's pricing page, Nano Banana 2.1 costs about three cents per image through the API at standard resolution, roughly half of what Nano Banana 2 cost. That matters if you build apps or generate hundreds of images. If you edit a few pictures a week in the Gemini app, you will not notice the price. The quality scores come from Google's own model card, so test it on your own images before you decide it is better.

## Your exercise for this week

Take one of your own photos, or generate one, and make three edits in a row: change a color, remove one object, add one object. Then repeat the same three edits, this time adding the sentence "Keep everything else exactly the same", and compare the two results. That comparison will teach you more than any video.
