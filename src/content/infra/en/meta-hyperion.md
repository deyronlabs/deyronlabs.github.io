## Hyperion, Richland Parish

Meta announced Hyperion in December 2024, on the 2,250-acre Franklin Farm site in Richland Parish, Louisiana. The first plan was a $10 billion campus of four million square feet. Meta describes the parish as a community of about 20,000 people.

## The numbers changed

In July 2026, Meta said the investment would pass $50 billion and the campus would grow to nearly 10 million square feet, with capacity rising from two gigawatts to five. A Meta spokesperson told Quartz the project should reach two gigawatts by 2030 and that there is no timeline for the full five. Bloomberg reported that the $50 billion figure does not include the chips, and that with the chips the price tag would be about $250 billion.

## What five gigawatts means

Five gigawatts is the computing load, the power drawn by the servers. Cooling and power conversion come on top of that. Tom's Hardware, citing Bloomberg, reports that about five gigawatts goes to compute and the rest to the wider campus, and that the power supply being built for the site is above seven gigawatts. For scale, one gigawatt is the usage of several hundred thousand homes, according to SiliconANGLE.

## Ten gas plants for one campus

The electricity comes from Entergy Louisiana. In 2025, regulators approved three gas plants, about 2.3 gigawatts. In March 2026, Entergy announced seven more combined-cycle gas plants of more than 5,200 megawatts, about 240 miles of 500-kilovolt lines, and batteries at three sites. That makes ten gas plants for one data center. Meta says it pays the full cost of the energy it uses. Entergy estimates about $2.65 billion in total customer benefits over 20 years from the two agreements.

## Why gas?

A combined-cycle plant burns gas in a turbine, then uses the hot exhaust to make steam for a second turbine. It runs day and night, and it can be built faster than most alternatives. A data center that runs around the clock needs power that is always there. Meta also points to nuclear uprates, including Waterford 3, and says it will help bring up to 2.5 gigawatts of clean energy. Those are Meta's own plans.

## Who pays?

The two sides give different answers. Meta and Entergy say customers will save money. But a consultant to the Louisiana Public Service Commission estimated in June that the deal could raise Entergy Louisiana customers' bills by $8 to $13 a month on average, as reported by the Louisiana Illuminator. These are estimates, not final bills.

## What can be checked

In August, a judge ordered Meta to explain how it calculated its job and power figures. On August 12, the commission voted 3 to 1 to overrule that order. Meta argues the information is a trade secret. Groups including the Sierra Club and the Union of Concerned Scientists wanted it released, and advocates have appealed. Until then, the headline numbers come from Meta, Entergy and state officials, not from the calculations behind them.

## The costs moved

In November, WWNO reported that the first of the new gas plants, Waterford 5, is now projected at $1.755 billion, up from $1.079 billion, an increase of 63 percent. The whole Laidley project, the plants and lines, is now $4.36 billion, up from $3.956 billion filed. This is a preliminary estimate. A consumer advocate told WWNO such estimates can be off by 10 to 40 percent. Entergy did not respond to WWNO's request for comment.

## The next vote

Entergy is asking the commission to approve the seven additional plants and their transmission lines in December, under a fast-track process called the Lightning Directive, adopted 4 to 1 in December 2025. On November 9, three groups sued the commission over how that process was adopted. Under Entergy's application, ratepayers would be responsible for at least $470 million in infrastructure.

## The people of the parish

Meta says the campus will provide more than 1,000 jobs once operational, and that local businesses have received more than $1.6 billion in contracts. It has pledged more than $1 billion for roads, water and wastewater, and $5 million for workforce training. A local chamber of commerce director told the Wall Street Journal the sales tax boost may be temporary.

## The pattern

This is the pattern we see at the largest sites. In Abilene, in Indiana with Amazon, and now in Louisiana, the real story is power: who builds it, who pays for it, and who gets to check the numbers. At Hyperion, the next answers come in December, and then in how much of the five gigawatts is actually built.

## What we're watching

The commission's December vote on the seven additional plants and their transmission lines, the outcome of the appeal over Meta's calculations and of the lawsuit over the Lightning Directive, and how much of the five gigawatts is actually built.
