## In short

- In September 2025 the Saline Township board voted 4–1 against rezoning farmland for a data center. The developer sued, the township settled under a court-approved agreement, and construction began on The Barn, a Stargate campus for OpenAI and Oracle.
- The companies say the campus has three buildings of 550,000 square feet each on 250 acres, with more than 1 GW of capacity. DTE says its contracts cover 1.4 GW. Epoch AI estimates 1.4 GW at completion and about 2.9 million H100-equivalent chips; that chip figure is an estimate.
- The Michigan Public Service Commission conditionally approved DTE's two special contracts in December 2025. In August 2026 Attorney General Dana Nessel and several environmental groups filed briefs challenging that approval in the Michigan Court of Appeals. We found no ruling as of 8 November 2026.

## How we work

We stick to documented facts, and we say whose numbers they are: the companies', regulators', a research group's estimates, or press reports. The video's voice and visuals are AI-generated.

## Who is who

Related Digital, the data center arm of Related Companies, develops the campus. Oracle is the customer; Related says it is building the project exclusively for Oracle, as part of the Stargate partnership between OpenAI and Oracle. Blackstone-affiliated funds provided equity and funds managed by Pimco anchored the debt. Walbridge, a Detroit firm, is the general contractor. DTE Energy, Michigan's largest electric utility, supplies the power.

## The numbers

Company figures: three buildings, 550,000 square feet each, 250 acres, more than 1 GW. DTE: 1.4 GW of supply under its contracts. Epoch AI: 1.4 GW at completion, about 2.9 million H100-equivalent chips (estimate). Cost: about $7 billion at announcement; Bridge Michigan puts it at $16 billion now, and Tom's Hardware notes that no explanation has been given for the increase.

## How a no became a yes

The land was zoned agricultural. The board rejected the rezoning 4–1 in September 2025. Related Digital and the landowners sued, alleging exclusionary zoning. The township's attorneys said its zoning power was limited and that the board had little choice but to settle. According to Fortune, the settlement brought about $14 million in community benefits, more than ten times the township's roughly $1 million annual budget. Residents raised concerns about wells, runoff, diesel backup generators and noise; Related says those concerns are based on misinformation.

## What has been built

Epoch's satellite imagery from 12 March 2026 showed foundation work for the first building. On 1 June 2026 the companies held a launch ceremony with Governor Whitmer. They say all three buildings are under construction, the first is nearing completion, and more than 700 Michigan tradespeople are on site. Oracle's release says construction will create more than 2,500 union jobs and the campus will have more than 450 permanent on-site jobs. Epoch projects completion in the fourth quarter of 2028 and listed current capacity as zero as of April.

## The money

In April 2026, Related Digital and Blackstone announced financing. DCD reports $14 billion in bonds sold through Bank of America, of which Pimco bought $10 billion, with equity from Related Digital and Blackstone-associated funds. The developers also committed $10 million to expand the Saline Recreation Center, plus the $14 million in earlier local commitments. Oracle says the project will generate well over $150 million a year in tax revenue for almost two decades; that is the company's figure.

## The electricity

DTE's CEO, Joi Harris, said on an earnings call that a facility like this would use 1.4 GW, about a quarter of DTE's total energy load. The companies say the power comes from existing resources plus new battery storage the project pays for, and that the project pays for all the electricity it uses. In December 2025 the Michigan Public Service Commission conditionally approved two special contracts without a contested case. DTE and the commission say existing customers will save about $300 million. The Michigan Environmental Council says the batteries would draw on DTE's existing mix, including gas and coal.

## The appeal

In August 2026, Attorney General Nessel, and separately Earthjustice, the Sierra Club, the Michigan Environmental Council and the Natural Resources Defense Council, filed briefs in the Michigan Court of Appeals. Nessel argues the application glossed over categories of cost and relied on heavily redacted contracts. The environmental groups cite nearly half a billion dollars in up-front costs to customers. The commission's spokesperson says it reviewed the unredacted contracts and that the approval has strong consumer protections. DTE says it is confident in its contracts.

## The calculation

Our arithmetic, not a company number: 1.4 GW running flat out for 8,760 hours is about 12.3 TWh a year, roughly what 1,136,000 average U.S. homes use at 10,791 kWh a year (U.S. EIA). Real use depends on how much of the capacity the campus actually draws. DTE's CEO also said any data center demand beyond this project would need new generation, for a one-gigawatt project a combined cycle gas plant.

## The ripple

According to Bridge Michigan, as reported by Tom's Hardware, at least 19 Michigan municipalities have enacted data center moratoriums. Washtenaw County commissioners passed a resolution supporting local moratoriums. A bipartisan bill proposes a one-year statewide pause, which Governor Whitmer and House Speaker Matt Hall oppose. Fortune reports residents started a recall effort against township board members. In March, OpenAI and Oracle said they would not expand their Abilene site, as DCD reported (see episode 2).

## What is real?

Supporters, including the governor, call it the largest investment in Michigan's history, with thousands of jobs and large tax revenue. Opponents see lost farmland and costs passed to customers. What is not in dispute is how it was decided: a township voted no, and the courts, the utility regulator and the financing decided yes. When you read about a data center, ask where the decision really sits: in a zoning board, a courtroom, or a rate case.

## What we're watching

Whether the Court of Appeals rules on the power contracts; whether Oracle announces the first building delivered, and when capacity actually turns on; and whether other Michigan proposals go forward.
