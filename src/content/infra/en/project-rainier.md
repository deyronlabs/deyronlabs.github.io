## In short

- Amazon says its New Carlisle, Indiana campus is expected to have 32 buildings across three parcels and is one of the homes of Project Rainier, the AI compute cluster where its partner Anthropic builds and runs Claude. On the northern campus, seven of nine buildings are operational.
- Amazon says it has invested more than $13.8 billion in the campus, and that under a 2025 agreement it pays for the power lines, plants and equipment needed to serve its data centers.
- Amazon did not give the campus's capacity. Epoch AI, a research group, reads public air permit documents for buildings 19 through 32 as pointing to total facility power near 2.3 GW.

## How we work

We stick to documented facts, and we say whose numbers they are. For this episode the figures come from Amazon and are labeled as Amazon's. Where Amazon gave no figure, we say so and name the public source. The video's voice and visuals are AI-generated, and we use Claude by Anthropic.

## Who is who

Amazon Web Services builds and operates the campus. Anthropic, the company behind Claude, is the partner running workloads on it. Amazon describes Project Rainier as one of the world's largest AI compute clusters: nearly half a million Trainium2 chips, the AI chips Amazon designs itself, deployed less than a year after the project was announced, and more than five times the compute Anthropic used to train its previous models.

## How Project Rainier is built

Project Rainier is an EC2 UltraCluster of Trainium2 UltraServers. An UltraServer combines four physical servers of sixteen Trainium2 chips each, linked by NeuronLink: sixty-four chips working as one, which Amazon says is 70 percent larger than any other AI computing platform in AWS history. Amazon says Claude now runs on more than one million Trainium2 chips across multiple U.S. data centers, for both training and inference.

## What is running today

Amazon says construction is progressing on schedule. On the northern campus, seven of nine buildings are operational and two are under active construction, on track to come online in the first half of 2027. On the southern campus, eight of nine buildings are expected to become operational in the coming weeks, with the last one targeted for early 2027. Overall the campus is nearly 50 percent operationally active, with more to build across all three parcels.

## The build-out

Construction continues with thousands of construction jobs and four general contractors: Clayco, Ryan Construction, Suffolk Construction and Walbridge. The drone footage in the episode was filmed by Amazon in 2025.

## The money

Amazon says it has invested more than $13.8 billion in the campus, surpassing the original $11 billion goal, with construction still underway. Across Indiana it cites more than $40 billion since 2010 in infrastructure, technology and employee pay, with $33 billion committed to data centers in the state, and estimates it has contributed $35 billion to Indiana's GDP.

## The electricity

Amazon says the campus is powered by the local grid, supplied by NIPSCO. Under a first-of-its-kind agreement announced in 2025, Amazon pays fees to use existing power lines and covers the cost of any new power plants, lines or equipment needed to serve its data centers. By the company's estimate the agreement is expected to save NIPSCO customers about $1.25 billion over fifteen years.

## Energy sources and utility bills

Amazon says it has invested in five wind and solar projects in Indiana totaling 635 MW, enough for about 146,000 homes, and that all the electricity its operations consumed was matched with renewable energy in 2023 and 2024. It announced, with X-energy and American Electric Power, a small modular reactor partnership in Indiana of about 960 MW. In May 2026, Indiana Michigan Power announced a 3.6 percent cut to utility bills, driven by load growth from large users such as data centers; Amazon is funding $2.5 million in bill assistance and efficiency programs. The campus capacity is the number Amazon did not give; per Epoch AI, air permit documents for buildings 19 through 32 point to total facility power near 2.3 GW.

## Backup generators

Amazon draws a line between what is permitted and what is emitted. It says the generators sit idle more than 99.9 percent of the time, running about ten hours a year on average, mostly for testing and maintenance. They are Tier 4 engines, which Amazon says cut nitrogen oxide emissions by 90 percent and other air pollutants by 70 percent compared with older technology, and its actual emissions are a small fraction of what is permitted.

## Water

Amazon says the campus uses direct evaporative cooling, relying on outside air about 98 percent of the year; on the hottest days, air passes through a water-soaked material and cools as the water evaporates. Once construction is complete, it expects about 18 million gallons of cooling water in an average year, 0.9 percent of the water the Town of New Carlisle produced in 2024. That estimate covers cooling only.

## Cooling and water infrastructure

Amazon says this approach uses 25–35 percent less electricity than chiller-based cooling at peak summer demand, and that a water recycling system comes online in 2027. It gives its water use efficiency as 0.12 liters per kilowatt-hour, against an industry average of 0.84 and 0.08 in Indiana. It is investing $114 million in local water and sewer infrastructure: four miles of new water main, four miles of sewer lines, three lift stations, an elevated storage tank and treatment plant upgrades, fully funded by Amazon at no cost to local ratepayers.

## Water replenishment

Amazon says it has funded three replenishment projects in northern Indiana, expected to conserve or improve the quality of more than 200 million gallons of water a year: sensors on 7,800 acres of farmland to cut groundwater withdrawals from the Kankakee Aquifer, smart wetlands that filter farm runoff into the Kankakee River watershed, and about 425 acres of restored habitat along Bogus Run.

## The people

Amazon says the campus has created more than 1,000 jobs so far, including more than 800 Hoosiers, plus thousands of construction jobs. A Campus Administration Building opened on site in June with training facilities and a work-based learning program; its first graduating class of 58 local residents with no prior tech background completed training in machine learning and data center operations.

## Step back

Amazon says it employs more than 27,000 people in Indiana and has helped create more than 40,000 indirect jobs. When you read about a data center, ask what is running, what is still going up, and who is paying for the power.
